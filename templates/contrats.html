Fin de contrat : des obligations pour les employés et les employeurs Contract termination: obligations for both employees and employers

La mise à terme d’un contrat implique des obligations pour l’employeur·euse et ouvre des droits pour l’employé·e. Ceux-ci diffèrent d’abord selon le type de contrat : [schéma contrat à durée déterminée vs indéterminée]. The termination of an employment contract entails obligations for the employer and grants rights to the employee. These differ primarily depending on the type of contract: [fixed-term versus permanent contract diagram].

Dans la suite, nous nous concentrons sur les contrats à durée indéterminée (CDI), which do not specify an end date. In the following sections, we focus on permanent contracts (CDI), which do not specify an end date.

Les motifs de rupture des CDI Grounds for termination of permanent contracts

Il existe de nombreux motifs de rupture d’un CDI, à l’initiative de l’employeur·euse, de l’employé·e ou d’un commun accord. La démission, la rupture conventionnelle, le départ à la retraite et les licenciements sont les plus fréquents. Ces motifs n’ouvrent pas les mêmes droits aux salarié·es. Permanent contracts may be terminated for various reasons, at the initiative of the employer, the employee, or by mutual agreement. Resignation, mutual agreement termination, retirement, and dismissal are the most common. These grounds do not grant the same rights to employees.

Les ruptures conventionnelles Mutual agreement terminations

La rupture conventionnelle is a procedure that allows employers and employees to mutually agree on the termination of a permanent contract and its conditions. It was introduced to facilitate amicable separations and allows employees to receive unemployment benefits. A mutual agreement termination is a procedure that allows employers and employees to jointly agree on the termination of a permanent contract and its conditions. It was introduced to facilitate amicable separations and grants employees access to unemployment benefits.
